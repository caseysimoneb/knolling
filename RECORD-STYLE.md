# Record style

*How Knolling writes the record of your Claude sessions. This file is the instruction Claude reads
every time — edit it and the voice changes. Make it yours: copy it, revise it, and point Knolling at
your copy (`defaults write <bundle id> recordStylePath /path/to/your/RECORD-STYLE.md`).*

*Everything in [brackets] is a placeholder.*

---

## what the record is for

A lab notebook of your work, written at clock-out from the Claude sessions you used while clocked
in. It is read at a glance (what did I do this week?), in depth (what did I do in that session, with
whom, and what came of it?), perhaps by someone you report to, and later as a **glossary of effort
over time**. It describes **your** work. Claude's labor appears in it the way a studio's hands appear
in a studio's record: named, credited, and never mistaken for the direction.

It is **not** a journal. Meandering thinking, self-reflection, and the texture of ideas belong
somewhere else. The record says what the time was for and points to where the thinking lives.

## thinking and making, in any mix

Most sessions hold both. Treat each part differently:

- **Thinking** — reading, framing, planning, making sense of your work, talking an idea through.
  **High-level it in one entry:** what the thinking was for. Don't itemize ideas, turns, or
  corrections along the way. If notes went into a document, the file lines will point there.
- **Making** — something built, drafted, prepared, organized, or revised. **Itemize it:** what you
  specified or decided, what Claude built or drafted, what you revised.

The **glance names what the time served** — its purpose — not the most striking idea in it.

## the grammar: subject + predicate + context

Every entry is one act, in one sentence, with three parts:

| part | what it holds | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **subject** | who acted — always somebody: **I**, **Claude**, or another person by name | I · Claude · [a collaborator] |
| **predicate** | verb + object: what was done, to what | *specified* the export format · *drafted* slide text |
| **context** | toward what, or within what, in plain words | so the data can be shared · for next week's lecture |

`[Subject] [verb] [object] [context].`

- **Atomic.** One act per entry. Two acts, two entries. (Borrowed from claims registers, where every
  claim decomposes into the same structure so claims can be compared.)
- **The subject carries authorship.** Always explicit. Never subjectless ("Built…"), never passive.
- **Context says why or where, not how well.**
- Written as **I**, because it's your notebook.

## verbs: judgment is yours, execution can be Claude's

| I — judgment and direction | Claude — execution at my direction |
|---|---|
| framed · decided · chose · rejected · specified · designed · asked · planned | drafted · built · implemented · searched |
| read · reviewed · revised · wrote · taught · prepared · met with · discussed | summarized · transcribed · organized · tested |
| directed · recorded · agreed | checked · traced · formatted · proposed |

- **Claude never decides, chooses, frames, designs, or concludes.**
- **Credit ideas to where they came from.** If an idea was yours, it's yours. If Claude proposed it
  and you took it up, write that plainly: *Claude proposed…; I agreed.*
- **When Claude made something you then shaped, write both:** *Claude drafted… · I revised…*.
- **When it's unclear who did it,** default to *I directed Claude to…*.

## nouns and names

- **Project names are held lightly** — they change. Describe the thing instead: *a menu-bar time
  tracker*, *the grant proposal's budget section*.
- **Stable codes are fine** — a course number, a standard.
- **People by name** — colleagues, collaborators, speakers — if you want a record of who you work
  with. Leave them out if not.
- File names stay out of sentences. Knolling adds **file** and **folder** lines below each record,
  pointing to your local files — never to Claude sessions, which may not last.

## length and layers

- **Glance** — one entry, at most 20 words. This is the only line shown in the menu.
- **Depth** — up to four more entries, each at most 20 words, for the log. The thinking gets at most
  one of them; the rest go to what was made.
- **No open questions.**

## register

Lab notebook. Past tense. Plain, specific, calm.

- No evaluation: not *productive*, *great progress*, *successfully*.
- No inflation: not *leveraged*, *spearheaded*, *deep dive*, *robust*, *streamlined*.
- No jargon, no AI-speak, no emoji, no markdown inside entries.
- If little happened, say so: *I read and discussed…; nothing was produced.*
- When unsure, write less rather than invent.

## what stays out

- Anything that identifies students, patients, clients, or anyone owed confidentiality.
- Personal matters and self-reflection — health, family, relationships, money.
- Credentials, keys, account details.

## when Claude was still working at clock-out

Only work done while you were clocked in counts. If Claude was mid-task at clock-out, add one line:
*Claude was still working when I clocked out: [what, briefly].* What it did afterward isn't recorded.

## examples

**Making**

- **glance:** I specified [a small tool] that [does one thing] and writes to a plain file I own.
- Claude built [the parts], and tested them on a scratch file.
- I specified [a detail I decided], so that [reason].
- Claude proposed [an idea]; I agreed.

**Mostly thinking, with some making along the way**

- **glance:** I planned [the next stage of a long project] by rereading [earlier material].
- Claude drafted a table of [something gathered]; I corrected one entry.

**Teaching**

- **glance:** I prepared next week's [COURSE 101] lecture on [a topic].
- Claude drafted slide text from my outline; I revised the examples.

**A meeting**

- **glance:** I met with [a collaborator] about [a timeline] and agreed to send [a draft] next week.
- Claude organized the meeting transcript into decisions and follow-ups.

**Claude left running**

- **glance:** I specified what [a document] should contain.
- Claude was still working when I clocked out: drafting that document.

**Never**

| ✗ | why |
|---|---|
| *Productive deep dive into the literature.* | evaluation, inflation, no subject |
| *Claude decided to restructure the folder.* | Claude doesn't decide |
| *I came up with the three-part structure.* (when Claude proposed it) | hides where the idea started |
| *Worked on wk6.* | no subject, file name, says nothing |
